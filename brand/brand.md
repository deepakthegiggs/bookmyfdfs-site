# FDFS brand and design system (decision 111, design phase, 25 Nov 2026)

Branch `design-system`. Nothing here is live. `main` is the live site.

**FDFS = Film · Deals · Friends · Seats** (decision 134, owner, 25 Nov; supersedes the "Date" in 042's Film · Date · Friends · Seats, 23 Nov). The site now leads with offers, so the D names the deals. 042 still stands on the other point: FDFS never means "first day first show". An earlier draft of this system read FDFS as a fan-ritual phrase. That reading is retired everywhere: in the copy, the logo, the motion and the "What is FDFS?" answer (see §11, round 3).

## 1. The story in one breath

**Film, deals, friends, seats: every movie plan comes down to four things. FDFS takes it from there.** Today that means the deals: which card or pass takes money off your tickets, checked against the official terms. Next comes what's new in your city and when booking opens.

The product the four words point to isn't announced yet. Until launch, the site keeps the promise small and true: offers, checked.

## 2. "What is FDFS?" (site copy, also the AEO answer)

> **FDFS stands for the four things every movie plan comes down to:** which film, which deal, who's coming, and where you sit.
>
> This site starts with the deals: ticket offers, each checked against the official terms.

**The four beats, applied to offers** (the tiles on the home page, built by `beat_tiles()` in `gen/fdfs_site.py`). Each tile is a fact computed from the offers that pass our checks at build time, never typed by hand. On 25 Nov 2026 they read:

| Beat | What it means for your offer |
|---|---|
| **Film** | Pick your film on BookMyShow and District: that's where these 5 offers work. |
| **Deals** | 5 checked movie offers. Highest cap ₹750: HSBC Premier Mastercard on BookMyShow. |
| **Friends** | Bring a friend: all 5 offers need 2+ tickets. |
| **Seats** | Caps run ₹200–₹750. A premium seat can cost more than the cap. |

With no movie offer, the tiles don't render. The Friends tile counts offers that are "2nd ticket free" or need 2+ tickets, and says "Every offer here works for a single ticket" when none do. (Before 134 the second tile was **Date**: "HSBC's offer needs a Saturday booking"; the Saturday fact now lives in the hero answer and the day strip.)

**Search target: branded only (owner, 25 Nov).** "What is FDFS?" and `/fdfs` aim at "FDFS offers", "bookmyfdfs" and "FDFS Film Deals Friends Seats". They don't compete for the generic "FDFS meaning". Titles lead with the brand ("FDFS · Movie ticket offers, checked 25 Nov 2026", "FDFS · New this week in Chennai"). Meta descriptions open with "FDFS (Film · Deals · Friends · Seats)". `WebSite` JSON-LD carries `alternateName`: FDFS offers, bookmyfdfs, FDFS Film Deals Friends Seats.

**Why these colours.**
- Indigo-night with marigold is the cinema itself: a dark hall and a lit screen. It's warm, festive and Indian, without borrowing anyone's colours.
- They stay clear of every brand we must not resemble:
  - BookMyShow's red and slate;
  - District's black and white;
  - PVR INOX yellow-on-black (marigold is warmer and sits on indigo, not black);
  - Paytm's blue;
  - bank reds and burgundies (HSBC, Axis, IndusInd) and ICICI's orange-and-maroon pair.
- Red is avoided entirely, so nothing on the page reads as a sale or a warning.

**Contrast.** `python3 brand/tools/contrast.py` checks every text pair in both themes, and all pass AA (≥ 4.5:1). The lowest pair is light `--text-3` on `--surface-2` at 5.35:1. Marigold-500 is never used for text on light: it is 1.9:1 there, so `--marigold-800` (6.3:1) is used instead.

**Theming.**
- Light is the default, and dark follows the OS.
- `<html data-theme="light|dark">` overrides it. The toggle stores the choice per browser, and `?theme=` sets it for screenshots.
- The hall (header and hero) is dark in both themes, and the answer "screen" is cream in both. That is the brand moment: a lit screen in a dark hall.

## 5. Type: Owner pick 25 Nov: B (Bricolage Grotesque for headings, Google Sans for reading)

**Frozen for S1** (the static generator build). System A (Archivo + Hanken Grotesk) was trialled on the real pages and not chosen. Its fonts and the `?type=a` switch are removed from the build. The side-by-side is kept as the record: `brand/records/type-a-vs-b.png`.

**Licensing, verified on fonts.google.com** (`fonts.google.com/metadata/fonts/<family>`, 25 Nov 2026):
- **Google Sans** (by Google) is `license: ofl`. Axes: wght 400–700, opsz 17–18, GRAD −50–200.
- **Google Sans Flex** is also `license: ofl`. Axes: wght 1–1000, wdth 25–151, opsz, slnt, ROND.
- **Bricolage Grotesque** (by Mathieu Triay) is `license: ofl`. Axes: opsz 12–96, wdth 75–100, wght 200–800.

All three are served by the Google Fonts API. So Google Sans is web-licensed, and no substitute was needed.

**Why Google Sans and not Flex.**
- Google Sans is the face the owner named.
- Its text build has a fixed optical size, tuned for reading.
- With only the weight axis kept, the Latin subset is **23 KB**. Flex's wght axis alone is 50 KB before subsetting, and we don't need its width or roundness axes.

| Role | Family | Settings | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Headings h1–h4, big values, poster titles, wordmark | **Bricolage Grotesque** | h1/h2 700, h3/h4 600; `font-optical-sizing: auto` (the opsz axis follows the size, so large headings get the tighter, crisper display cut); tracking −0.02 em (−0.01 for h3/h4); never all caps; line-height 1.08 for h1, 1.15 for h2 | It has character (ink traps, a lively "a" and "g") without shouting. Restrained weights keep it classy, not loud. |
| Body, UI, labels, numbers in running text | **Google Sans** | 400 for text, 500 for labels and chips, 600–700 for emphasis; 17 px at 1.6 line-height; measure ≤ 68 ch | Open, friendly and very readable at small sizes, with clear figures for prices and times. It's a calm partner for a characterful display face. |
| Small caps labels (eyebrows, legends, tags) | Google Sans | 12–13 px, 500–700, caps, +0.08 em | Quiet structure |
| Codes (AXISSELECT) | system monospace | 700, +0.06 em | No download |
| Tamil, Telugu, Devanagari, Malayalam, Kannada | **Anek** siblings | in every font stack after the Latin face (`--indic` in tokens.css) | Matched across Indian scripts. Pages fetch them with `&text=` subsetting, only when they carry that script. |

**₹.** Neither Latin subset has ₹. Each family gets a ~1.3 KB ₹-only file, cut from its own latin-ext file and loaded through `unicode-range: U+20B9`. So a ₹ inside a Bricolage heading is Bricolage's ₹, and a ₹ in body text is Google Sans's ₹.

**Budget** (first load, self-hosted, subset to Basic Latin + punctuation, layout features kept: kern, liga, calt, tnum, lnum, case):

| File | Bytes |
|---|---|
| `google-sans-latin.woff2` (wght 400–700) | 23,076 |
| `bricolage-latin.woff2` (wght 600–800, opsz 12–96) | 43,164 |
| `google-sans-rupee.woff2` + `bricolage-rupee.woff2` | 2,544 |
| **Total** | **68.8 KB** (target ≤ 120 KB) |

- Only the two Latin files are preloaded, both with `font-display: swap`.
- Metric-matched fallbacks (Arial with size-adjust 101.9 % for Google Sans and 99.3 % for Bricolage, computed from real glyph advances) keep the swap from reflowing lines (CLS ≈ 0).
- **Specimen:** `preview/shots/type.png` shows h1–h4, body, small, caption, ₹ figures, and Tamil and Hindi samples.

**Scale** (fluid, 360 → 1280 px):

| Step | Size |
|---|---|
| Caption | 13 |
| Small | 15 |
| **Body** | **17** |
| h3 | 20→24 |
| h2 | 22–24 phone, 26→36 from 520 px |
| h1 | 26–28 phone (2 lines), 32→52 from 520 px |
| Poster | 40→72 |

Bricolage is wider than the old condensed face, so display sizes came down about 15 %.


### The A vs B trial (record)

- **A:** Archivo (width axis) + Hanken Grotesk. 48.3 KB of fonts, and a 2-line phone hero.
- **B:** Bricolage Grotesque + Google Sans. 68.8 KB of fonts.
- **Result:** the owner picked **B** on 25 Nov, on my recommendation. Bricolage's optical sizing reads editorial and warm. A's expanded Archivo read sporty and fintech. Hanken also lacks ₹ and small caps in its Google Fonts build.
- **What B borrowed from A: a 2-line phone hero.**
  - Below 520 px the h1 is `min(7.2vw, 1.75rem)`, i.e. 28 px at 390 and 26 px at 360, at line-height 1.12. "Movie ticket offers for your plan, checked today." is **2 lines at 360 and 390 px** (measured in the DOM).
  - To keep the step below it, h2 drops to `min(6.2vw, 1.5rem)` (24 px) and "What is FDFS?" to 28 px.

- Sheet: `brand/records/type-a-vs-b.png`. The logo A vs B record is `brand/records/logo-a-vs-b.png`.

## 6. Motion

There is one moment of motion, and it spells the brand: **four beats, then the lights.**
- **The four beats:** the kicker FILM · DEALS · FRIENDS · SEATS lights up one word at a time (0.25 s apart, 0.6 s each). The first letters stay marigold, so the eye reads F-D-F-S.
- **The seats in the header mark** light up left to right in the same rhythm.
- **The projector beam** fades in once with a single flicker (1.6 s), then stays still. It's cinema warmth, not meaning.
- **Hero text** rises 12 px. **Cards** lift 2 px on hover, on devices that can hover only.
- **Never:**
  - no loops;
  - no marquees;
  - no countdowns;
  - no pulsing "live" dot. A pulse reads as urgency.
- **`prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`:** every animation sits inside `no-preference`, so reduced motion shows the final, fully lit state at once. The duration tokens are also 0.

## 7. Voice

The voice comes from `docs/fdfs/voice-and-copy.md` (decision 118), adapted for the web.
- **A friend who's good at getting tickets.** Warm, quick, and sure only where we're sure.
- **Lead with the answer:**
  - "7 movie offers pass our checks."
  - "Booking on Sat 28 Nov? HSBC credit cards: 2nd ticket free, up to ₹250."
- **Numbers beat adjectives:** "Twice a month per card", "First 2,000 cardholders each Saturday".
- **Honesty rules (CCPA dark-patterns guidelines):**
  - always **"up to ₹X"**;
  - always **"Terms checked <date>"** with a link to the official page;
  - never "you save ₹X" or an effective price;
  - no "hurry", no countdowns, no false scarcity;
  - expired offers stay on the page, marked as expired;
  - unknowns are said out loud: "No end date stated", "No day limit stated in the terms", "Not shown yet: no terms page found".
- **Plain words:** "Booking open", "Not open yet", "Open at some cinemas". Not "availability detected".

## 8. Do's and don'ts

**Do:**
- show the cap, the one condition that trips people up, the checked date and the source on every offer card;
- use the curved-screen motif for the one most important box on a page;
- spell the four beats in this order, with interpuncts: Film · Deals · Friends · Seats;
- keep marigold for one job per view (the accent), and use ink for everything else;
- design typographic, poster-free film cards: the release date in outline, the native-script title, and the English title. No TMDB or studio images (TMDB's terms ban commercial use without an agreement);
- write "Independent. Not affiliated with BookMyShow, District, any cinema or any bank." in every footer.

**Don't:**
- use bank, UPI, app or cinema logos, colours or trade dress. Names appear as plain text only, to say where an offer applies (nominative use; Trade Marks Act s.30);
- expand FDFS as anything other than Film · Deals · Friends · Seats: not the old "Film · Date" (retired by 134), and never "first day first show" (042);
- build a showtimes page (seats.movie owns that). Our seat content is per-premium-screen guides;
- use ticket stubs, 🎟 as the mark, red sale badges, or "BOOK NOW" buttons (we don't sell tickets);
- use film posters or stills (copyright);
- mention the unannounced product or its channel before launch;
- say "best offer". Sort order is stated plainly ("highest cap first") and isn't a recommendation;
- use urgency: no countdowns, pulses or "only N left".

## 10. Legal guardrails applied (research round, 25 Nov)

1. **Names as plain text only:** banks, UPI apps, apps and cinemas. There are no logos anywhere.
2. **Poster-free:** no posters, stills or TMDB images.
3. **Lead with FDFS everywhere.** Nothing echoes BookMyShow's colours or look, and the non-affiliation footer is on every page.
4. **Affiliate links:** none exist. If one ever does, the disclosure sits right next to it. There's no false urgency. Every "checked" claim shows its date and time plus the source link.
5. **The booking-open tracker** is labelled "Coming soon". The ticket sites' terms ban automated reads, so the source is being decided.
6. **Analytics:** cookieless only, with no banner.

```bash
U="U+0020-007E,U+00A0,U+00A9,U+00B7,U+00D7,U+00E9,U+2013,U+2014,U+2018,U+2019,U+201C,U+201D,U+2022,U+2026,U+2192,U+2193,U+2197,U+2713"

#   ₹ files: the same subset with --unicodes=U+20B9 on each family's latin-ext file

```

## 11. Design-director critique log (what I changed after looking)

**Round 0, the logo sheet.**
- A was a flat rounded rectangle with a sun. It read as a generic weather icon.
- I redrew the screen as a *curved cinema screen* (sagging top, lifted bottom). That made it ownable, and gave the system its motif.
- The sun is clipped to the curved edge, so it sits on a horizon.
- The favicon gets 3 heavier rays and a bigger sun for 16 px.

**Round 1, the first full renders.**
1. **The 390 px shots were fake.** Headless Chrome enforces a minimum window of about 500 px, so the first mobile shots were a cropped 500 px layout. I now render inside an iframe at the exact width, and trim to the content height.
2. **Hero beam:** it was a thin sliver that died before reaching the screen. I widened the cone (56° instead of 40°), raised its strength and lengthened the fade, so it visibly lands on the answer box.
3. **Value line:** the cap was inline on some cards and wrapped on others. It now always sits on its own line under the big value, so the cards scan as one column.
4. **Seat glyph:** the empty seat was invisible on light. It now uses `--text-3` at low opacity, and the free seat is marigold.
5. **The orphaned 7th card** on desktop (3 + 3 + 1): the "Also checked: live events" block moved into the grid and spans 2 columns at ≥ 1080 px. The rows are now full. The block hides whenever a filter is active.

**Round 2.**
1. **Mobile hero pushed offers below the fold** (about 780 px). I tightened the padding, made the lede smaller, and hid the 3-number tally under 520 px, because the answer sentence already says it. Filters now start at about 690 px, and the answer box is fully visible in the first screen.
2. **Posters had an empty top half.** I added the release day as huge outlined numerals with the weekday and month ("27 FRI NOV"). The date is the information the user wants, and it makes each card a typographic poster without artwork. While doing this I found a bug: `color: transparent` also made `currentColor` in the stroke transparent. I switched to `-webkit-text-fill-color`.
3. **"Also checked" block:** it was a hollow box on desktop. It now has a big "10%" value on the left, like an offer card.
4. **Mini posters** were uneven widths on mobile. They are now fixed 150 px tracks.
5. **"New this week" eyebrow** wrapped and orphaned its dot. I shortened it to "Week of 27 Nov · checked 11:30 IST" and balanced the wrapping.
6. **Pill legend** on mobile: each pill and its explanation now stack.

## 12. Redesign round 1: the home page answers "what can I use?" (26 Nov 2026)

Built from the deal-site research (patterns 1–7, 9, 10, 11). Nothing here changes what counts as checked.

- **Value-first card.** Who (with a monogram tile), the money line ("2nd ticket free up to ₹750"), one row of condition chips (day · app · tickets · limit · the spend gate · validity), the dated check with its source, then Details and Share. Terms, the quote and "Cite this" sit one tap away in a native `<details>`. The chip that trips people up (a spend gate, a booking day, a paid pass) carries the marigold rule.
- **Spotted cards** use the same bones and never look checked: a dashed slate border, a "👀 Spotted · not verified" pill, "Spotted on <site>" where a checked card says "Checked by", and a dashed "Code (unverified)". Checked codes are solid.
- **Monograms**, not logos: 2–3 letters on one of five tints mixed from our own palette by a stable hash. Never a bank's colours.
- **Validity as words**, never a timer: "Saturdays only · next Sat 28 Nov", "Ends in 5 days · 31 Dec", "Last day · 31 Dec", "No end date". The browser recomputes them for the visitor's IST day.
- **Search as you type** over what the page already shows: "hdfc sat bms" reads as HDFC Bank · Sat · BookMyShow, shown as removable chips. It knows bank aliases, apps, days in English and Hinglish (aaj, kal, parso, shanivar…), "weekend", UPI apps, codes and buy-1-get-1 words. Leftover words match the card text.
- **My cards**: pick banks; saved in this browser only (localStorage, `fdfs-cards`), never in the URL, never sent. With cards set, the list and the answer follow them ("With your Axis Bank + HSBC: 3 checked offers for Wed 25 Nov. Highest cap ₹750 (…)"). Without storage it works until the page closes, and says so.
- **Live counts on every chip and day**; a chip the other filters empty is greyed, never hidden. A sticky "Showing / Searching" bar lists every filter with ✕, Clear all, and "Share this list" (WhatsApp, with the filtered URL).
- **URL state**: `?q=&bank=&app=&day=&type=` round-trips; the canonical stays on `/`.
- **Phones**: Type and bank sit in a bottom sheet ("Filters", with "Show N offers"). Desktop and no-JS show them inline.
- **No JS**: the full static list, CSS radio filters and the static answer sentence (what crawlers read). Search, My cards and the bar only appear when they can work.
- **Budget**: HTML + CSS ≤ 20 KB gzip per page as before; the home script has its own cap (≤ 16 KB, ≤ 6.5 KB gzip). Dead preview-only CSS was removed to make room.
